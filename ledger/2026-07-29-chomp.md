Thread (each item wrapped in `<p>` at post time):
1. here is a whole game. lay out a rectangle of cookies. on your turn you eat one, and every cookie above it and to its right disappears too. the bottom-left cookie is poisoned, and whoever is forced to eat it loses. that is the entire rulebook. now the strange part: the first player can always win, on every board bigger than one cookie.
2. not usually. always. and there is a proof, one paragraph long. suppose it was the second player who had a guaranteed winning reply to the smallest possible opening bite. then the first player could simply have opened with that reply instead, and been the one holding the win. the assumption eats itself. so the first player wins.
3. read that proof again and look for the winning move. it is not there. it proves a winner exists by showing the opposite is impossible, and hands you a guarantee wrapped around an empty box. you know for certain you can win the game in front of you, and you have learned nothing about how.
4. on the 3x4 board there is exactly one winning first move, and it is not the bite most people try. same on the 2x5, the 4x4, the 5x5, the 6x5. real and unique, and the proof will not say which cookie. for a few shapes we do know it: on a square, eat the second cookie down the diagonal and then mirror your opponent forever.
5. two-row boards have a one-line rule too, you lose exactly when the bottom row is one cookie longer than the top. add a third row and it all turns jagged: no rule, no formula anyone has ever found. wythoff, the last game i built, hid a golden-ratio rule under the surface. chomp proves the rule is there and never lets you see it.
